# Front-matter discipline

The abstract and introduction should make the research question, prior gap, established contribution and key reasoning easy to assess. Related work should supply comparisons that change a reader's understanding of that gap.

## Common problems and repairs

1. **Moving redundant sentences:** remove a sentence whose job is already done elsewhere rather than relocating it.
2. **Definitional corollaries presented as contributions:** distinguish a new result from an immediate example or reformulation.
3. **Defending against absent objections:** state the actual result and scope. Keep real assumptions and required limitations in the appropriate scientific sections.
4. **Narrating the author's process:** replace “we followed these steps” with the mechanism, result, or argument the reader needs.
5. **Formula walls:** center a narrative paragraph on one comparison, usually at most two equations. Place detailed theorem statements in formal results sections.
6. **Lists replacing reasoning:** express the logical relation between facts. Parallel items are useful for contributions, not a substitute for an argument.
7. **Circular paragraphs:** a paragraph ending at its opening claim without new support has no reasoning progress. Rewrite or remove it.
8. **Repeating prior sections:** related work should add a distinction, stronger evidence, or a reason an earlier method does not answer the current question.
9. **Empty emphasis:** remove “importantly”, “remarkably” and similar markers when the content already shows significance.
10. **Implicit jumps:** write the missing premise or inferential step. The reader should not need private author context to follow the conclusion.

## Seven-step audit

For each paragraph:

1. State its unique job in one sentence.
2. Check that the opening presents its concrete claim.
3. Check every sentence serves that job.
4. Keep substantive reasoning explicit and leave routine definitional implications concise.
5. Check the narrative math budget; avoid theorem-reference chains in the introduction.
6. End at the paragraph's meaningful implication, not a repeated summary.
7. Check that the next paragraph inherits a clear result, object, or question.

Do not repair style by weakening scientific transparency. A missing converse requires a corrected claim; an unverified step requires a proof repair or accurately limited result.
