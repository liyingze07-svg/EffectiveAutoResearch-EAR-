# Research-writing quality checklist

Each sentence should supply new, precise information or a necessary reasoning step. Use these seven dimensions as an independent writing audit; they are general criteria, not an imitation of a particular person's preferences.

1. **Terminology:** one concept has one established name. Define unfamiliar terms after explaining their meaning; avoid invented labels that merely rename a familiar object.
2. **Mechanism:** explain how a claimed effect follows. Replace “X enables Y” with the specific mathematical or algorithmic relationship and evidence.
3. **Information gain:** identify what a reader loses if a sentence, table or paragraph is removed. Cut duplicates and decorative summaries.
4. **Structure:** lead each paragraph with its concrete claim, then give the necessary evidence and reasoning. Order sections by logical dependency.
5. **Claim strength:** match quantifiers, constants, scope and strength words to the proved result. Distinguish sufficiency, necessity, examples, separation and full characterization.
6. **Citation closure:** every cited result exists, supports the stated claim and has matching hypotheses. Every “this/both/these” has a clear referent.
7. **Reader reasoning:** expose substantive inferential steps, while leaving routine definitional implications implicit. Check that the narrative is understandable without prior author context.

For an audit finding, return file, exact locator, severity, the violated dimension, and a concrete repair. Auditors report; the coordinator edits. Scientific accuracy takes precedence over a more persuasive presentation.
