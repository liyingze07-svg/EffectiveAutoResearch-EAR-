# Scientific claims and scope

An introduction should state the actual result, its evidence and why it matters. Avoid defensive repetition about objections the paper never raised; use the space for the argument and its scientific scope.

Do not turn this into a policy of hiding weaknesses. Required limitations, proof gaps, assumptions, failed verification, computational-only evidence and informal/formal status remain accurate and visible in the appropriate scientific location. If an unsupported claim needs a disclaimer to survive, repair or downgrade the claim.

## Audit distinctions

- **Redundant apology:** “We do not claim to solve every related problem.” If the paper never made that claim, cut the sentence and state its actual result.
- **Required scope:** “Under bounded losses, the rate is …” Keep the assumption in the theorem and setup, and in claims that depend on it.
- **Unresolved proof step:** Do not erase the caveat. Fix the proof, remove the unsupported theorem, or label the verified partial result.
- **Repeated future-work confession:** Replace a vague list with a precise open question when it contributes to the discussion; do not frame an unperformed check as performed.
- **Method provenance/status:** Keep experiment provenance and verification status in their meaningful locations. Do not imply a simulation is a real review decision or an informal proof is machine verified.

After each revision, inspect changed claims against sources and Harden. A rhetoric grep can identify text for review, but a zero-hit grep is not a license to remove required scientific disclosures. Return exact locators and suggested rewrites; the coordinator decides and records changes.
