# Sentence and paragraph checks

For every sentence:

1. Use the paper's established terminology.
2. Ask what information disappears if it is cut; remove sentences with no useful increment.
3. Give every pronoun and cross-reference a clear referent.
4. Match the wording's strength to the result's evidence and scope.
5. Put the paragraph's conclusion before its elaboration.

For every paragraph:

6. Do not repeat a heading as a decorative opening sentence.
7. Use tables for comparisons; do not restate every entry in prose.
8. Keep critical evidence next to the claim it supports.

For reviewer responses:

9. Answer the actual question directly, including yes/no when appropriate.
10. Use the reviewer's terminology so the response maps to the concern.
11. Explain the disputed step rather than teaching a reviewer concepts they already invoked.
12. Supply a concrete manuscript change or verification for each resolved finding.
13. Do not claim that a concern is resolved without evidence.

Apply these checks after drafting, before the independent review. Prefer a surgical correction over rewriting unaffected sections.
